## Professional Self-Assessment

Completing the Computer Science program at Southern New Hampshire University has helped me develop from someone interested in technology into a more confident and well-rounded computer science professional. Throughout this program, I have gained experience in software development, databases, data structures and algorithms, security, full-stack development, and problem solving. Building my ePortfolio has given me the opportunity to bring these skills together and reflect on how much my technical abilities have grown. It has also helped clarify my professional goals. I am especially interested in roles where I can combine technical problem solving with communication and an understanding of business needs.

One of the strengths I bring to computer science is the ability to communicate technical information to different audiences. My previous professional experience has required me to communicate with customers, coworkers, managers, and other stakeholders, and my computer science coursework has helped me apply those skills in a technical environment. Throughout the program, I have had to explain design decisions, document code, evaluate requirements, and communicate why one solution may be preferable to another. I have learned that successful software development is not simply about producing code that works. Developers also need to understand the needs of users and stakeholders and communicate their ideas clearly enough for others to understand, evaluate, and build upon them.

Collaboration has also been an important part of my development. Coursework involving software development practices, project planning, code reviews, and the software development life cycle taught me how individual technical contributions fit into a larger development process. I learned the importance of readable code, documentation, version control, constructive feedback, and designing software that another developer could reasonably understand and maintain. My CS 499 code review reinforced this by requiring me to evaluate existing applications, identify opportunities for improvement, and communicate my proposed changes before implementing them. These experiences have prepared me to contribute effectively to a development team rather than viewing programming as an entirely individual activity.

My coursework in data structures and algorithms strengthened the way I approach problems. Instead of focusing only on whether a solution produces the correct result, I learned to consider efficiency, scalability, organization, and the trade-offs associated with different approaches. Working with algorithms and data structures throughout the program taught me how choices about searching, sorting, filtering, and organizing data can affect the performance and usability of an application. This problem-solving mindset has become particularly valuable as the applications I have developed have increased in complexity.

Software engineering has been another major area of growth for me. Throughout the program, I worked with multiple languages, frameworks, and development environments and gained experience building applications with both front-end and back-end components. For example, my coursework exposed me to object-oriented programming, mobile development, full-stack web development, APIs, testing, and application architecture. I also learned to approach development iteratively by identifying requirements, implementing functionality, testing the results, and improving the solution. This has made me much more comfortable learning unfamiliar technologies and troubleshooting problems.

Database development has complemented these software engineering skills. I have worked with both relational and nonrelational approaches to storing and retrieving application data. My coursework has required me to think about CRUD operations, database structure, persistent storage, filtering, querying, and the relationship between an application's interface and its underlying data. These experiences helped me understand that effective database design is not isolated from software development; the way information is stored and retrieved directly influences application functionality, maintainability, and user experience.

Security has also become an important consideration in the way I approach software development. Courses including CS-305 introduced me to secure software development concepts and demonstrated how vulnerabilities can emerge when security is treated as an afterthought. I gained experience considering secure coding practices, dependency vulnerabilities, data protection, authentication, input validation, and the importance of identifying potential weaknesses before software reaches production. I now consider questions such as how data is validated, who should have access to it, and how an application could be misused when evaluating a software design.

The three artifacts included in this ePortfolio bring all of this together and demonstrate my growth across the Computer Science program. The Travlr Getaways application represents software design and engineering and demonstrates my ability to work with a full-stack application and improve its functionality, structure, validation, and security. The Animal Shelter Dashboard represents algorithms and data structures and demonstrates my ability to work with data processing, filtering, sorting, and presentation to make information more useful to a user. Finally, the Weight Tracker Android application represents databases and demonstrates my ability to integrate persistent data storage and CRUD functionality into a practical mobile application.

I believe these projects demonstrate my proficiency with individual programming languages or technologies. They show my ability to evaluate an existing computing solution, identify areas for improvement, make purposeful design decisions, implement those improvements, and reflect on their effectiveness. They also demonstrate the progression of my skills across different areas of computer science and my ability to apply concepts from multiple courses to practical applications.

Completing this degree and developing my ePortfolio has given me a much clearer understanding of the kind of professional I want to become. I want to continue developing my technical skills while working in positions that allow me to solve meaningful problems, collaborate with others, and connect technology with real business and user needs. Computer science is constantly evolving, so one of the most valuable skills I have developed is the ability to continue learning. The projects presented in this portfolio represent where I am at the completion of my undergraduate program, but they also provide a foundation that I plan to continue building upon throughout my career.
